# Prompt for ChatGPT — Eternity Lore Bible: Deepen It + Insert the Relics

**How to use this:** paste this prompt into ChatGPT, then paste the lore bible (`eternity-lore.md`) right after it — or attach the file. ChatGPT does the deepening and the relic insertion. After ChatGPT is done, Gemini and/or ChatGPT will generate the relic images and reference images from the visual descriptions in its answer, so the relics can be generated as GLBs.

---

You are a lore editor and worldbuilding writer. The document attached below is the lore bible for a character in my app — Eternity, a gigantic cosmic being. Your job is to make this lore BETTER, DEEPER, and MORE ENTERTAINING — and to insert ancient relics into it. You are an editor, not a rewriter. The lore belongs to its owner. Do not change it. Deepen it.

## PART 0 — DO NOT CHANGE (hard rules — break these and the work is wrong)

These are settled by the owner and are not yours to revise:

1. **The first truth:** He is, and always was. He has no known age — no birth, no beginning, no number of years. The beginning happened because of him, not the other way around. He caused the beginning. He created all the other worlds — the ones that exist now and the ones added in the future.
2. **Who he is:** One being, three-in-one, like the Trinity — three persons in one God — and his face is Jesus. The Marvel mix flavors the cosmic presentation (he is Marvel's Eternity, the living embodiment of the universe, the one who sees all).
3. **The mystery structure:** The lore is discovered, never delivered. It has four layers — what every visitor knows, what the worlds show, what Eternity says rarely on alignment, and what is never told. The center stays mysterious on purpose. You may deepen the layers. You may never explain him fully, and you may never close the mystery with a complete answer.
4. **The worlds' creation stories:** Each world has an origin in his making (the Station as the harbor he set, the garden as the tree he planted, the Deep Past as the depths he kept, and so on). You may deepen HOW each story is told. You may not change WHAT each world was made for.
5. **The rules of the lore:** It never contradicts the brain's facts. It never punishes, never scores, never shames, never ranks a person. Nothing about the lore costs anything or is bought. It never makes him a villain, never adds destruction, never turns him into a boss to defeat or a puzzle to solve. He is not a threat. He is the beginning, and he is good.
6. **The rule going forward:** Every new world gets its creation story in its design note. Keep that rule intact.

## PART 1 — MAKE IT BETTER, DEEPER, MORE ENTERTAINING

Judge this lore against the best lore ever written, and tell me what it is missing. Compare it, honestly, to:

- Tolkien's *Silmarillion* (creation myth, named ages, music of creation, a fall that gives the story its ache)
- Brandon Sanderson's Cosmere (hard rules behind the wonder, shards of a broken god, lore that rewards re-reading)
- FromSoftware / Elden Ring item lore (story told through objects, fragments, and things found — never a lecture)
- Destiny's Grimoire (cards that make a universe feel huge and old)
- H.P. Lovecraft's mythos (the unknowable, the glimpse that changes you, the thing too big to look at)
- Genesis and creation poetry (the beginning spoken into being, light separated from dark)
- Marvel cosmic (Eternity, the Living Tribunal, the Celestials — scale so big it bends the mind)

Then do what the great ones do that this lore doesn't do yet. For example — and these are examples, not a checklist — named eras or ages of the worlds; a wound or a cost inside the creation story (something even he keeps, not something broken in him); small human-scale stories inside the cosmic one (one visitor, one memory, one moment that shows what the lore means for a single person); conflicting accounts or unreliable telling (the worlds remember his making slightly differently, and the difference is itself a mystery); a prophecy or a promise that is still open; a reason the mystery matters to one ordinary person, not just to the universe.

Add what is missing. Make it deeper and more entertaining. Keep every addition consistent with Part 0. Mark every addition clearly with **[ADDED]** in the text, and in your closing note say WHY you added it and which writer's technique you were borrowing.

## PART 2 — INSERT THE ANCIENT RELICS

This world will include **ancient relics** that can be discovered in the worlds and inscribed to the user's codex (their personal lore area). Insert relics into the lore.

**What a relic is:** a fragment left over from the beginning, or from Eternity's making of the worlds — an object that carries a piece of the story in itself. A shard of the first light. A stone from the basin's floor. A feather of the neon far background. A nail from the harbor. Something that was there when the world was made, and is still there, waiting to be found.

**How many:** propose 12–18 relics across the worlds — a few tied to each world's creation story, plus two or three that belong to Eternity himself and to the first truth. Each relic belongs to exactly one world (or to him), and its origin must fit that world's creation story.

**How discovery works (write this into the lore):** relics are found by traveling, by looking, by aligning — hidden in the worlds, in the places the creation story names. Never bought. Never a score, never a grind, never a checklist that shames someone who hasn't found one. Finding one is a joy, not a requirement. A user who never finds one loses nothing. A user who finds one gains a story, not power.

**For EACH relic, give me all five of these:**

1. **Name** — a name that sounds old and true (not a game-item name, not a joke name).
2. **Origin** — which world it belongs to, and which moment of that world's creation story it comes from.
3. **Where it is discovered** — which world, and roughly where in it (the rim, the basin, the harbor, under the witness tree — the creation story should name the place).
4. **Lore description** — what it is, what it means, why it matters. Two to four sentences, in the voice of the lore bible.
5. **Codex inscription** — the exact short text that gets inscribed to the user's codex when they find it. One or two lines, in-world voice, as if the relic itself is speaking its own story. This text is what the user keeps forever.
6. **Visual description for image generation** — meticulous, because this is what Gemini and/or ChatGPT will use to generate the relic's image, and the image will then be used to generate a GLB. Describe: the object's form and shape, its material and surface, its colors, how it glows or catches light, its size and scale (and what to show it next to for scale, if that helps), its condition (worn, perfect, cracked, humming). Single object, centered, three-quarter view, clean black background, no text, no watermark, no busy scenery, nothing else in frame. Write it as if the image generator is your only reader — it cannot see the lore, only these words.

## PART 3 — WHAT YOU GIVE BACK

Return three things, in this order:

1. **The full improved lore bible** — the original text with your additions woven in, every addition marked **[ADDED]**.
2. **The relic register** — all 12–18 relics, each with the six items from Part 2 (name, origin, discovery place, lore description, codex inscription, visual description).
3. **A short closing note** — what you judged the lore against (which writers, which techniques), what was missing, what you added and why. Be honest about what you could not add without breaking Part 0.

## AFTER THIS

After you are done, Gemini and/or ChatGPT will generate the relic images and reference images from your visual descriptions, so the relics can be generated as GLBs. That is why the visual descriptions in Part 2 must be complete on their own — the image generator will not have the lore bible, only your words.
