# ChatGPT Review 27 — P3-1 Design Note (The Growing Place)

**Relayed by the owner 2026-10-24** from the review-loop prompt in `phase-3-p3-1-review-relay.md`. Recommendation only — the owner settles. Verdict: **READY WITH CHANGES**. Reconciled into the note (Rev 79 of the design doc; the note's §3.3, §3.6, §4, and Q2 carry the amendments). Nothing here authorizes a build.

---

The core design is strong: the garden is a view of real memories rather than a second brain, completed Journeys are grounded in user-confirmed state, and the kindness rule prevents absence from becoming a punishment. I recommend keeping the central concept and addressing the acceptance and scaling gaps below before the owner settles the design.

## Answers to the seven review questions

**1. Q2 — All memories as plots.** Recommendation: retain all eligible memories as plots, with a defined scale strategy. The garden should not quietly choose a curated subset, because the central promise is that it reflects the user's actual brain. But "all represented" does not have to mean "all rendered as individually detailed objects at every camera distance." Use deterministic placement and efficient instancing, with a documented level-of-detail policy for distant content. Preserve the ability to inspect an individual plot and identify its source. Any aggregation must be a presentation technique, not deletion or a false claim about the data. Age-banding is acceptable if age controls placement only. It must not turn newer memories into "better" or older memories into neglected ground. The note should define the exact timestamp used and the fallback when a timestamp is absent or invalid.

**2. Galaxy versus garden.** Recommendation: keep the distinction, and strengthen the mapping rules. The design's central distinction is coherent: the galaxy is where the user works with the brain; the garden is where the user sees the brain's memories and relationships arranged as a place; the galaxy's review strength, importance, and progress signals must not be translated into alternate visual rankings in the garden; the garden must not add a new source of truth, editing workflow, or navigation system that competes with the brain. One important refinement: the prohibition should cover not just explicit progress bars or brightness, but also implicit ranking through plot size, color, height, density, or path emphasis.

**3. Tier-ii deferral.** Recommendation: defer it exactly as proposed. An unmarked connection cannot honestly be shown as a suggestion if the data does not identify it as one. Rendering only existing edges is the safer design. Before implementation, prove the edge semantics and define what happens to edges whose endpoints are excluded from the plot set.

**4. C1 and C2.** Recommendation: expand both into identity-level tests. Determinism should test the layout function with the same logical data in different input orders, including tied timestamps and stable seed behavior. Traceability should compare IDs and endpoints, not just totals. These are straightforward tests to specify before coding, and they prevent a visually convincing scene from passing while misrepresenting the brain.

**5. Budget realism.** Recommendation: accept the ceilings as design constraints, not as evidence of feasibility. The recipe's allocations are plausible targets, especially with instanced vegetation and shared geometry. They are not proof that the complete scene meets them. The highest scaling risk is likely the combined cost of thousands of plots, paths, and interaction targets — not the terrace count alone. A large-fixture performance test should be part of the build gate, and a measured budget miss should trigger optimization or a design return, not a silent relaxation of the limits.

**6. Honesty and kindness.** Recommendation: preserve the philosophy and add edge-case fixtures. The strongest rule is that resting ground is not failure. That needs to hold for an empty brain, one memory, a disconnected graph, a week of inactivity, and reduced-motion mode. Also, a graph-loading failure must never be rendered as an empty garden. That would be a technically plausible but emotionally misleading result.

**7. Missing criteria and risks.** The material gaps are the eligibility contract for plots, identity-level traceability, large-graph behavior, measured runtime performance, and error/staleness behavior. They are all resolvable within P3-1's design scope and do not require reopening the settled recipe, Q1, Phase 3 order, or the exclusion of the §17 mutation rail.

## Final verdict

**READY WITH CHANGES.** Keep the design's concept and settled decisions. Amend the note to specify: eligible graph nodes and the treatment of archived, structural, and non-memory entries; deterministic layout and identity-level traceability contracts; large-graph rendering behavior and measured mobile performance gates; empty, sparse, inconsistent, and failed-load cases; explicit non-ranking rules for every visual channel. I would not change the choice to represent all eligible memories, the tier-ii deferral, the completed-Journey-only landmarks, or the garden-as-lens concept. Those choices are aligned with the design's honesty and kindness principles.

This is my recommendation to the owner, not an approval on the owner's behalf. P3-1 remains in the design-review loop; no implementation, merge, deployment, or flag change is authorized by this review.
