# Fleet Defense — Concept Note (DRAFT)

**Status:** CONCEPT — owner's direction, 2026-10-25 (voice). No design settlement, no build authorized.
**Source direction (owner, 2026-10-25):** depending on a person's cognitive function, emotional state, and the thresholds they meet within each tab of the app, they can attract a fleet of ships to their brain from other worlds. The reasons get fleshed out over time. Example: something negative attracts a fleet coming to purge — purge a memory, purge the emotion of a memory, purge poor finances, whatever the tab is. Soumaya and her fleet play a space-defender / Space Invaders-type game: the ships fly around your galaxy, around your second-brain space, and Soumaya and her fleet have to defend. She is autonomous and does it on her own, or you jump in and take control and play it. Defending yourself is okay with Eternity. Find a way to make it super entertaining.

## §1 The loop in one paragraph

Something in your brain crosses a threshold (see §2) → a fleet arrives from another world, coming to purge the negative thing → Soumaya and her fleet defend your galaxy in a Space Invaders-style defense → she plays it autonomously, or you take the helm and play it yourself → the negative charge is cleared and what matters is kept.

## §2 Triggers — tab thresholds

- Every tab in the app (memories, goals, emotions and mental state, finances, and the rest) carries **thresholds**: plain, visible conditions the owner defines. Examples only — the owner settles the real ones: a goal attacked three days running; a memory carried with negative weight for a month; a debt falling below a mark; a skill practiced to a milestone.
- Meeting a threshold **contributes to the worlds**: it feeds the mutating world (it grows), it feeds Alignment, and it can summon an event — including a fleet.
- Thresholds are **never hidden judgments and never gate access**. They open opportunities; they never close doors. A hard week changes nothing about what you can reach.

## §3 The invaders — what a "purge" is allowed to mean

- The invading fleet comes to purge **a negative thing**: the emotion of a memory, a stale pattern, a debt being worked down — whatever the tab is.
- **Hard line, stated plainly:** a purge fleet **never deletes brain data**. The brain is authoritative and the worlds layer is disposable — a world cannot destroy what the brain holds. "Purge" in the game means clearing a **charge the user already chose to release** (an emotion they want let go of, a memory they archived, a debt they are paying down). The fleet takes only what was offered. Anything the user wants to keep is defended, never purged.
- This is the design tension, named rather than hidden: purging a memory would be loss, and loss is forbidden. The game is about **defending what matters while letting go of what was already released** — not about destroying anything.

## §4 The defense — Soumaya and her fleet

- **Space-defender play:** invader ships fly around your galaxy / second-brain space; Soumaya's fleet meets them. The shape is Space Invaders at its simplest — incoming ships, defensive fire, positions that matter — inside the real galaxy view, not a separate game screen.
- **Two modes, same game:** (a) **Autonomous** — Soumaya plays it herself, on her own, as the agent she is. You watch your defender work. (b) **Take the helm** — you jump in and control the defense yourself. Switching between the two is free and instant; neither mode is "the real one."
- **Winning:** the negative charge is cleared and what matters is kept. **Losing:** nothing is lost. The fleet disperses, the charge stays, and the defense can be tried again later. There is no punishment for losing — losing is a quiet "not yet," never a penalty.
- **Eternity's stance:** defending yourself is okay with Eternity (owner's words). He watches; he does not intervene in the defense.

## §5 Relation to existing systems

- **D-55 fleets:** Soumaya dispatching fleets on automated missions, bounded, with end-game costs — the defense fleet is one such mission, and the same bounds apply.
- **§16 ship-agent queue:** Soumaya acts as a fenced world-actions-only sub-agent; the defense runs inside that fence (allow-listed world actions, budgeted, propose→decide for anything structural).
- **Fuel economy:** PROPOSED — defending your own brain is **free** (home defense); taking a fleet out to another world costs fuel. Owner settles.
- **Relic buffs:** relics may affect defense (e.g. Hearth Ember steadies the defense, Star Hanger aligns it) — open, settled with the buffs note.
- **The mutating world:** the world that grows over time grows from the same tab thresholds (§2). Fleet events are one of the things that make it grow.

## §6 Assets — real ships, no stand-ins

- Defender ships: Soumaya's own ship (`soumaya-ship.glb` already in the app) + ships from the free-ship shortlist (`docs/worlds/free-spaceship-shortlist.md` — Kenney Space Kit / Space Kit, Quaternius Ultimate Spaceships Pack, all CC0, no account needed).
- Invader ships: a **visually distinct** set from the same shortlist (PROPOSED: Quaternius fighters as invaders, Kenney craft as defenders — owner picks).
- **Standing rule (§8 of the buffs note):** real assets, measured, licensed, slimmed, render-checked. A streak of light that is "supposed to represent" a ship is a placeholder, never the shipped thing.

- **Q6** Costs and rewards: does defense cost fuel? Does winning earn anything (fuel, a bonus, a codex entry)?

- **Q8** Eternity's role: does he ever do anything in a defense, or only watch?

## §8 What this is NOT

- Not a second game bolted onto the app. The defense happens inside the galaxy you already have.
- Not a progression ladder. No levels, no XP, no ranks.
- Not a punishment system. Nothing is ever taken for a bad week, a lost defense, or a negative memory.
