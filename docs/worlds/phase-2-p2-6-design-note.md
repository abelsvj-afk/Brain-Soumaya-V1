## 3. Design

### 3.1 Scale — make it gigantic (the owner's direction)

Scale is felt in sequence, not stated (recipe §2). The moves:
- **Approach distances stretched:** the three stages (far 900 → handoff 380 → arrival 150) are re-measured against the hull's real size so the Station overfills the view on final approach and the home sun reads as a backdrop disc the ring can eclipse (the recipe's awe-scale rule). The exact numbers are build-recon work against the Profondo hull's measured dimensions.
- **Ship-to-station ratio made explicit:** the user's ship (and every traffic ship) is sized against the wheel so the ratio reads at a glance — the Station houses ships, so ships must look housed. A fixed ratio (ship length : wheel diameter) is recorded in the layout module and test-asserted.
- **Witnesses in the middle distance:** a hauler and a drydock frame share the frame on the approach (recipe §2) so the Station's size always has something honest beside it. **Correction from recon:** the Kenney Station Kit is interior furniture — it supplies neither. The hauler is a Kenney Space Kit craft (`craft_cargoA/B`, measured); the drydock frame is composed from Space Kit pieces (hangar_largeA + platforms + structures, itemized in the ledger) or built procedurally per §3.2. Station Kit pieces are docking-arm dressing only, where they genuinely fit.
- **Docking arms with docked ships of many sizes:** arms crowded with moored ships, cargo pods, fuel lines — the Station reads as a working port, not a monument. Sizes vary so the scale ladder is visible in one look.
- **Objective acceptance (review 29, B-3).** "Overfills the view" and "reads at a glance" are not acceptance language on their own. C1 is evaluated from the recorded approach as follows: at the final-approach framing condition (the arrival-arc camera position, Station centered per the recipe's composition rule — never dead-on until the final arc), the Station's silhouette covers the full frame height; and in a defined middle-distance frame (the handoff stage), a scale witness (hauler or drydock) is visible beside the Station. The final measured hull dimensions, the resulting approach-stage distances, and the approved ratio are recorded in the build evidence. The changes must preserve the settled crown-and-stem silhouette, four-arm form language, amber/indigo identity, and the recipe's middle-distance witness composition.
- **Q1 SETTLED (owner's delegation, 2026-10-25; King's pick as a real game, from the recon measurements): ratio 35:1** (wheel diameter 420 / ship forward length 11.95 = 35.15), **approach stages UNCHANGED — far 900, handoff 380, arrival 150** (re-measurement confirms them: far 34.3% frame height, handoff 78.9%, arrival 164.9% — overfills, C1 met), **resting orbit 1250 → 600 (height 240 → 120)** (station covers ~51% of the frame at rest instead of ~25%; 110 units clearance to the outermost traffic lane at radius 490 — builder verifies gate/traffic clearance at build). The Station reads as a city, not a ship: a 12-unit shuttle beside a 420-unit wheel is a boat beside a harbor wall. **Build requirement (recon finding): `approachPose` is dead code today** — defined and tested but called by nothing; the Station sits in a fixed 1250 orbit, which is why it does not feel gigantic. The build **must** wire A13: the arrival sequence flies `approachPose` (900 → 380 → 150) on arrival, then settles into the 600 orbit. Without this, the stage numbers stay decoration.

### 3.3 Animation — better, not busier

- **A17 traffic lanes:** continuous, ambient; reduced motion halves density with no near-lane passes.
- **A13 approach:** the three-stage closing, with the handoff graded for pop-in/flicker/depth artifacts (the recipe's named technical risk, §11.3 V3).
- **R-S4 ring rotation:** slow and real, stately enough never to compete with route information; reduced motion suppresses visible rotation while preserving the same scene information.
- **New, small, in-scope:** docking sequences (a ship easing onto an arm), blinking work lights, a slow orbit reveal on first arrival (A15). **Out of scope:** any animation that implies a user, a brain event, or a reward — traffic implies no users and no brain activity (§14.2).
- **Animation boundary (review 29, B-4).** Docking is a **presentation-only ambient animation**: no persistent docking state, no ship schedules, no simulated users, no new travel or state-machine behavior. The orbit reveal stays within the existing A15 first-arrival contract: skippable, not repeated unless requested, with a reduced-motion equivalent. The §14.1 fields are completed for each new behavior — trigger, actors, movement, duration, reduced-motion equivalent, cost, and user controls (skip, cancel, interruption, repeat): docking (trigger: ambient, continuous; actors: one traffic ship + one arm; movement: slow ease onto the arm and hold; duration: sequence; reduced motion: ship appears docked with a soft fade, no motion; cost: cheap; controls: none needed — ambient, interruptible by leaving the world); work lights (trigger: ambient; actors: world; movement: slow blink rhythm; duration: continuous; reduced motion: steady lit, no blink; cost: cheap; controls: none); first-arrival orbit reveal (trigger: first ever visit; actors: ship, world; movement: slow orbit of the crown before the arc; duration: journey; reduced motion: the glide with one extra fade stage, per A15; cost: heavy; controls: skippable, not repeated unless requested). All three are included in the combined worst-case load and fallback accounting. Reduced motion preserves the same scene information and arrival behavior, not merely hides the effects. The "traffic implies no users" rule stays explicit.
- **Fallback ladder (in order):** traffic count → nebula plane count → passing-scenery density → approach particle haze. **Never degraded:** the station silhouette and its LOD handoff, guidance lights, route information, or the arrival itself.

### 3.4 Performance ledger — measured, not asserted

At build recon the builder measures every candidate asset (glTF-Transform inspect, same as Profondo) and records a ledger: Profondo 132,432 (known) + dressing + traffic + ships + backdrop + UI, with the total landing **materially below** 750k tris and ≤88 draw calls. Any asset that would push the total to the boundary is cut or LOD'd — the boundary is a warning, not a target. The ledger is filed in the build record and the scene module test-asserts the constants.
- **Internal targets (review 29, B-1 — SETTLED by owner's delegation, 2026-10-25; King's pick as a real game): internal triangle target ≤500,000 visible tris** (a 250,000-tri reserve against the 750,000 hard rejection boundary, held for uncertainty and combined-scene load) and **internal draw-call target ≤64** (24 calls of headroom against the ≤88 allowance). The recon ledger (Variant A, all measured) lands at ~189,500 tris / ~32 calls — 38% and 50% of the targets — so the targets hold with large margin. 750,000 tris / 88 calls remain the hard rejection boundaries — the internal targets are the numbers the build is judged against. **Measurements, not estimates:** the ledger uses measured counts for the actual assets and the worst-case visible scene configurations (closest approach, full traffic, all dressing visible) — never estimates from low-poly style or pack descriptions. **Fallback, predetermined:** if the selected assets exceed an internal target, remove or replace lower-priority accessories, reduce their measured visible geometry, or return to design. Never quietly consume the reserve, never weaken the visual requirements to force a pass. **Hull call correction (recon):** the hull is 5 draw calls (5 material groups), not 4 as the layout plan says — the ledger uses 5.
- **Ship assets:** the hero is **Soumaya's own ship, `soumaya-ship.glb`** — already in the app (`graph/soumaya.ts`, flying in the home galaxy), 8,558 tris, 10 meshes (builder measures the deployed file: bbox, materials, draw calls). She is scaled to a 12-unit forward length so the settled 35:1 ratio holds, oriented forward along −z. The existing loading/material treatment is reused; the procedural hull stays as the load-failure fallback. **Provenance (open audit item, pre-existing):** the file carries in-file copyright 'Modeled and Textured by 3DHaupt aka DennisH2010' with no license statement in the repo and no `CREDITS.md` entry — this increment does not newly introduce the file (it already ships), but making it the hero raises the stakes on closing the gap; record it as UNVERIFIED in the provenance inventory. Kenney `craft_cargoB` / `craft_miner` are traffic/dressing candidates only. Traffic ships are instanced; one InstancedMesh per model. **Quaternius textures are 2048×2048 and must be resized to 1024 before shipping** (§11). **OGA stylized spaceship is excluded** — no license file in the downloaded zip; under the B-2 rule a source-page CC0 claim alone does not pass. **Quaternius source:** the builder works from the Godot conversion (measured, AABBs verified against the stored .tscn values); prefer the native FBX if the Drive quota resets, and record the conversion in the provenance inventory either way.
- **Textures:** ≤1K (hero ≤2K), per §11. Kenney/Quaternius models are low-poly with small or no textures — verify at import.
- **Asset provenance (review 29, B-2 — the auditable gate).** The shortlist records license verification from source pages; this note requires verification from the file. The two standards reconcile as follows: the builder keeps an **inventory of every model actually selected for shipping** — source pack, exact model/file identity, license evidence, any required attribution or modifications note, measured triangle count, final asset identity. The downloaded archive's license/readme material is inspected and mapped to the selected models; a pack-level license covers multiple models **only if the evidence clearly establishes that coverage**. **The hard case, stated:** if the source page states CC0 but the downloaded files contain no license evidence, that asset does **not** silently pass on the source-page claim alone — it is excluded unless the owner clarifies an acceptable evidence standard. Only selected models are inventoried (inspecting all 240 Kenney files is unnecessary; sampling a few would not establish provenance). The credit record and the measured ledger correspond to the **actual shipped files**, including converted or modified GLBs — not just the original pack names.

### 3.5 Completeness — the garden-underside lesson, applied here

- The Station is a whole solid: Profondo hull + dressing read as a complete object from every angle the camera allows — including from below and behind. Any angle the builders cannot finish is outside the camera's allowed range, by construction — and the range is tested (see §4 C7).
- The approach corridor is bounded: the camera cannot leave the lane into unbuilt space.
- **Reproducible coverage (review 29, B-5).** "Drive the camera to every allowed extreme" is a test intention, not a test. The build records the actual camera-control limits of the WorldsSurface camera system and identifies every reachable boundary: minimum/maximum zoom or distance, orbit-angle extremes, allowed pan/target offsets, and approach-corridor limits. A repeatable coverage sweep is defined over those bounds — including relevant combinations of extremes — with evidence retained for each tested view. Scripted approach transitions and the reduced-motion path are included, since either can expose geometry or staging a static orbit misses. If a view cannot be completed, the camera is constrained so that view is genuinely unreachable, and the constraint itself is tested. An undocumented or untested angle is never counted as complete.

### 3.6 Loading, error, and staleness behavior

- Profondo load failure keeps the existing honest behavior (`noteModelFallback`, proxy hull renders). A dressing-asset failure degrades to the previous accessory level — never a crash, never a blank Station, never a silent missing piece presented as complete.
- No new server surface. No economy change. No state-machine change.
