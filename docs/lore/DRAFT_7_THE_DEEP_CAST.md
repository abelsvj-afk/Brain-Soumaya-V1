# DRAFT 7 — The Deep Cast: wounds, houses, ends, and the seams that let the story keep growing

*PROPOSED by Claude, 2026-10-31, from the owner's request: "like Elden Ring, every individual character or canon NPC had their own backstories and a lot of sad ones … an amazing spiderweb effect … relationships, past, trauma, good things, deaths … how the game could continue to make DLC … we don't want to change anything, we just need to add on top."*
**Status: nothing here is canon until the owner locks it.** This draft is **strictly additive**: it contradicts nothing in Drafts 1–6, the Continuity Bible or the shipped Book; every new fact is tagged **[NEW]** and every place it touches a locked fact it only *explains a silence*, never overwrites a word. Anything the owner dislikes can be struck without breaking the rest (each [NEW] is self-contained, and the checker in `shared/story.ts` still proves the web holds after any strike).

## 1. What Elden Ring actually does (and what I could and could not verify)
**Sources, honestly:** I ran web searches on the game's storytelling, NPC questlines and the *Shadow of the Erdtree* expansion. The results were mostly commentary and fan discussion, thin and uneven; I trust the three claims below because they recur across sources and match what I know of the game, and I mark everything else as my own reading, not a citation. Useful pointers if you want more: the [Game Developer piece on narrative design in Elden Ring](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/narrative-design-in-elden-ring), the [Shadow of the Erdtree story overviews at Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/elden-ring-shadow-of-the-erdtree-lore-messmer-miquella-1851492064) and [Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/games/elden-ring-shadow-of-the-erdtree-story-trailer-explained/), and the Elden Ring wiki's Lore page.

**Recurring across sources**
1. **The story is in the things.** Weapons, armour, talismans and consumables carry short passages about their makers, owners and the events they touched; the player assembles the story from item descriptions, oblique dialogue and the shape of places. The payoff is cumulative — enough fragments and the themes become unmistakable — and the studio declines to publish an official reading, so players argue.
2. **NPCs are quests that are also tragedies.** Their threads run in small steps across the whole world, can be missed, and many end in loss — a trusted companion turning against their will, a powerless sorcerer dying in a futile attempt to belong, a love that cannot cross a divide. One ending is the default for a player who follows none of them; the others are reached through different people.
3. **A hidden first act.** The expansion revealed a buried past behind the world's founding myth — a ruler's rise that came at a cost, a hero erased from history, a land that the main story had hidden under its central tree. It is released as a story that runs *alongside* the main one, not as a sequel — which is exactly why it could be added without breaking anything.

**My reading of why it works (labelled as mine)**
- **Every figure carries a wound, a want, a warmth, a debt and something withheld.** Not all five are shown to any one player; each is *implied* by objects and by what others say.
- **Relationships are the load-bearing walls.** The sad ones are sad because two people each hold half of something and never put it together. The player is the only place the halves meet.
- **Lineage and teacher/student chains** let one death echo three generations later.
- **One event, many tellers.** The disagreement *is* the lore.
- **The world is built on a grave.** The cheerful surface is a monument to something lost; the expansion works by lifting a corner of it.
- **Deaths are specific.** Named, placed, tied to an object and to someone who is still carrying it.

**What we already have that works this way:** the fifteen witnesses as people (Draft 3 §4), five contradicting accounts of the Rending, the Lateral Rule, Item Reactions, the web checker, the Flavour engine, and the physical spots. What we are missing is **depth per person** and **a map of who is tied to whom**. That is this draft.

## 2. The Rule of the Five (applied to every canon person)
For each person below: **Wound** (what happened to them) · **Want** (what they are doing about it) · **Warmth** (the small good thing in them) · **Debt** (who they owe or who owes them) · **Withheld** (the thing they will not say — never revealed in-game, only circled) · **Ties** (edges in the web) · **End** (what became of them, or what is coming — a death, a fading, a walking-in, or a rest).
The product rules stand: nothing here can be failed, lost or punished; a thread you leave *rests*; no one is a villain; no score, no count, no timer.

### 2.1 The Firstborn
**CALEND, the Doorkeeper (the Wayfarer).**
- **Wound:** he set the first nail for Teru and sent him toward the sealed seam; Teru never came back through. Calend has kept every door in the Lands since, in case one of them is the door Teru walked through. **[NEW]** *He does not know whether he sent Teru to his death or to what Teru wanted.*
- **Want:** to hold the one door that is *for* someone. **Warmth:** he keeps the nail warm with his own hand, every day, and tells no one that's why it is warm. **Debt:** to Teru, and **[NEW]** to **Mara Halvane**, Teru's sister, who still keeps a lamp lit at the Station gate-post and has never asked him a question. **Withheld:** how it ends (locked); and **[NEW]** that he once said *no* to a door.
- **Ties:** Teru (guilt) · Mara (silent debt) · Hesk (a closed door between them, §3) · the Cartographer (the lane) · the player ("I'll ask you to hold a door").
- **End:** *rests.* He is always already at the next door. **[NEW]** His coat has one unfinished patch; the colour it is waiting for is the player's.

**ORU, the Dawnkeeper.**
- **Wound:** the one who remembers everything is beginning to forget — and the first thing going is the Siege of the First Light. **Want:** to hand the First Morning on before it fades; he teaches recall for that reason. **Warmth:** he greets you by your real name. **Debt:** to **Orsolya First-Eye**, his student, who walked in holding a flake of the first light, and whom he *cannot now picture*. **Withheld:** what the Siege was for. **Ties:** Orsolya · Cael Waterline · the player (your recall holds him) · Sethane (they measured the first hour together).
- **End:** *he does not die.* **[NEW]** If the player recalls with him often, he is more himself; if not, he rests inside the bead of light he gave, and the bead is warm. Nothing is lost either way. (This ties the spaced-recall mechanic to a person.)

**SETHANE, the Measurer.**
- **Wound:** her measurements of the Hush all come out the same size as the grief of the Sixth Hour, and she stopped eating. **Want:** to be wrong. **Warmth:** she tells you your life in years and months and is gentle with the number. **Debt:** her apprentice, the Surveyor, who inherited the work without the cause. **Withheld:** the number. **Ties:** the Surveyor · Oru · Cael (the tears, counted) · the knot she never tied.
- **End:** **[NEW]** *no one saw her go.* Some say she went in to measure the hole from inside; her cord is left by the old station in the sky with its last knot untied "because it hasn't ended."

**MAREN, the Gardener.**
- **Wound:** her apprentice Iselle tried to grow *her* back from nothing and walked in carrying a dawn that never finishes. **Want:** to stop her. **Warmth:** she waters what can be named. **Debt:** to Iselle. **Withheld:** why she said *Do not water what you cannot name.* **Ties:** Iselle · Wend (who planted themself beneath the tree) · the Keeper (her heir).
- **End:** **[NEW]** *a winter.* She sat under the witness tree to wait out a hard winter and did not stand in the spring. The tree's dead bough is the one she was leaning on. The Keeper has never been told whether Maren left her the tree or the burden.

**NUARÉ, the Hearthkeeper.**
- **Wound:** warmth with no one to be warm *for*; every hearth she lays has one chair too many. **Want:** to fill it. **Warmth:** the cushion she carries between hearths for whoever arrives. **Debt:** to Tamsin, who carries her work. **Withheld:** whom the chair is for. **Ties:** Tamsin · Kith · Maud and Edric (her first couple) · the Open Hand.
- **End:** *she goes on laying the place.* **[NEW]** There is one hearth where the chair is never empty, and no one remembers who sits in it.

**VESH, the Hollow Mercy** *(they/them)*.
- Locked: erased the beloved; sympathetic, not a villain. **[NEW] Warmth:** they fold paper boats from Archive pages — each boat is a page of someone's grief that Vesh *could not bear to leave in the dark*. **Debt:** to **Noor Quillon**, the first archivist, who asked what rest was and was not answered. **Want:** to ask the made what *they* want, which they never did. **Withheld:** the verse; they cannot remember it, only that it was sung. **Ties:** the Archivist (their penitent) · Noor · the Lullaby's hollow · the Sleepwalker (§2.4).
- **End:** the hollow through the chest is *the same size as the hollow along the Lullaby's flank.* Neither is explained.

### 2.2 The Hive
**YSMEREL, the Last Choirmaster.**
- Locked: secretly a Sitter; the Choir cut a verse on purpose. **[NEW] Wound:** she did not cut it. Her predecessor **Aldous Fenn** *refused*, and was sung into silence by the Choir — the first Silence. The page torn from the hymnal is the one thing Aldous carried out; Ysmerel has never asked who tore it, because she has always known. **Want:** to hum the gap right once. **Warmth:** she will let a stranger sit in her stall. **Debt:** to Aldous. **Withheld:** that she is the one who finished the cut. **Ties:** Aldous · the Choir · Vesh (the missing verse is the same silence) · the Archive's open alcove (Aldous's).
- **End:** she sits at the edge of the quiet by night. **[NEW]** If you stay, she hums, once, the first note of the verse, and stops.

**DORROW QUINT, the Ledger's Grandmaster.**
- Locked: keeps the Great Book; owes a debt to "a person unrecorded"; pays through Wim. **[NEW] Wound:** the first line of that entry is in the founding charter's ink, in a hand no one can match — **and Dorrow cannot rule out that it is his own, from before.** **Want:** to settle it. **Warmth:** he has never once been thanked and has never asked. **Debt:** to the unrecorded one, and to Wim. **Withheld:** the unrecorded person is the one the made cannot name. **Ties:** Wim · the Ledger · Brannoch's berth · the player ("would you like to pay it?").
- **End:** *unsettled.* **[NEW]** The Book has one blank line at the bottom of every page, for a reason the binder insists was a printing error.

**HESK, the Warden-General.**
- Locked: enforces ordinances; keeps a locked room of belongings; polishes a tin soldier he cannot account for. **[NEW] Wound:** the tin soldier was his son **Pip's**. Pip was Faded at six. Hesk has forgotten him entirely, and cannot throw the toy away. **Want:** to hand *one* thing back. **Warmth:** he folds a stranger's coat. **Debt:** to Pip; and to Calend, whose door he once closed. **Withheld:** the name. **Ties:** Pip · the locked room · Tamsin's ward (never spoken) · Calend.
- **End:** he asks the player to keep one belonging safe, off the books. **[NEW]** It is always the tin soldier, and he always says he doesn't know whose it is.

**TAMSIN of the Open Hand.**
- **Wound:** her husband **Ruel Dane** was Faded and is returning *in pieces of whoever remembered him*; the pieces that carry the most of him are other people's. **Want:** to remember him enough to let him finish returning. **Warmth:** buttons in her pockets for anyone who has lost one. **Debt:** to Ruel; to Nuaré. **Withheld:** it hurts less when she doesn't remember him, and she has noticed that. **Ties:** Ruel · the ward · Nuaré · Kith (§3) · the player ("keep them in your memory").
- **End:** *continues.* **[NEW]** A patient with Ruel's laugh sits in the ward on days Tamsin is not there.

**OSSIAN REED, the Last Honest Liar.**
- **Wound:** his true story is the only one he never tells. **Want:** to be caught. **Warmth:** he tells you the wrong story on purpose so you'll find the right one. **Debt:** **[NEW]** to **Pell**, the Follower — he invented her as a character in a tale told at the Station, and then she turned up. **Withheld:** whether he made her or merely named her. **Ties:** Pell · the Tall Tale · the coin that isn't his.

**KITH, the Child Who Remembers Wrong.**
- **[NEW] Wound:** her mother **Linnet** is a Faded patient in Tamsin's ward; Linnet's coat hangs in Hesk's locked room; Kith's drawing of the faceless woman is her mother as the Faded do not look. **The three of them hold one person in thirds and have never met.** **Want:** to be remembered *for* — "I'm only small." **Warmth:** the extra place she lays at the table is for whoever is hungry. **Debt:** none; the world owes her. **Withheld:** she is not told. (Locked: *this is the thread the lore hangs on.*)
- **End:** *she is not told.* **[NEW]** Her great-grandmother was the one of forty-two who turned back at the door — the Forty-Second — and her line has been the one that *stays* ever since.

### 2.3 The Smaller Hands
**PELL, the Follower.** *Wound:* always one day behind. **[NEW]** She is walking in the late light of **Sahl's star**, which Sahl hung for "someone who might be walking." *Want:* to catch up, once. *Warmth:* she leaves a coin, a ribbon and a note for whoever is ahead. *Ties:* Sahl · Ossian · the player. *End:* the day she catches up, her light is no longer late.

**WIM OSTLER, Brannoch's clerk.** *Wound:* he keeps the berth-log of a ship that never existed. **[NEW]** He pays its fees himself, anonymously, every year, because Brannoch taught him that what is kept is not owed to you but *for* you. *Ties:* Brannoch · Dorrow (who knows, and says nothing) · the Mooring Payments. *End:* *continues.*

**ILSABET MARROW, the Cartographer.** *Wound:* she charted the hole's clean edge first. **[NEW]** The Dissenter's chart is *her* copy; they stopped speaking over whether to publish it. *Ties:* the Dissenter (rival, once close) · Calend (the lane) · the second seam. *End:* her lane ends at the seam; she has begun a lane beyond it.

**The DISSENTER.** *Wound:* he was right about the clean edge, and it did not help anyone. *Want:* to be argued with by someone who can win. *Warmth:* he agrees, rarely, and it is a gift. *Ties:* Ilsabet · the hole · "it is the only place that is not his."

**The SURVEYOR.** *Wound:* she inherited a measuring that ends in a number she cannot say. *Want:* to finish Sethane's sentence. *Warmth:* quiet delight at true sizes. *Ties:* Sethane. *End:* **[NEW]** at the Hush's edge she says Sethane's line, *"exactly the size of what we're missing,"* and does not know where it came from.

**The KEEPER.** *Wound:* heir to a garden and an instruction. *Ties:* Maren · Iselle · Wend · the dead bough. *End:* **[NEW]** the day the seed sprouts she will be the first to hear it and will say nothing for a year.

**The ARCHIVIST.** *Wound:* penitent to Vesh. *Want:* to be tempted and refuse once more. *Ties:* Vesh · the Folded One's letter (she holds it) · the open alcove. *End:* *she thanks you for the thing she needed most* if you don't open the fold.

**The SLEEPWALKER.** *Wound:* a Faded woman through whom the Hush speaks. **[NEW]** Before she was Faded she kept the lamp at the dock; the stranger who shared a cup with the player had shared one with her. *End:* at dawn she is only a woman, and does not know you; **[NEW]** she keeps the cup.

## 4. Houses and Lines (teacher → student → heir)
Seven **Lines of keeping** let a death echo down generations — and give the Book a *lineage* view later.
- **Line of the Dawn:** Oru → Orsolya (walked in) → *(no heir — Oru's gap)*.
- **Line of the Measure:** Sethane → the Surveyor; Cael Waterline measured beside them.
- **Line of the Door:** Calend → Teru (walked in) → Mara (the lamp) → *(the lamp is the heir)*.
- **Line of the Garden:** Maren → Iselle (walked in) / Wend → the Keeper.
- **Line of the Hearth:** Nuaré → Maud & Edric (walked in) → Tamsin → *(the Open Hand)*.
- **Line of the Rest:** Vesh → Noor (walked in) → the Archivist (penitent).
- **Line of the Choir:** Aldous (silenced) → Ysmerel → *(none: the Choir ends with her, or with whoever hums the gap)*.
Hesk, Dorrow, the Dissenter, Ilsabet, Ossian, Pell and Wim stand **between** lines — they are what the lines left behind.

## 5. One event, many tellers (new shared events, additive to the five accounts of the Rending)
Each event is told by **three** people who disagree on one thing, and none is wrong.
1. **The Cutting of the Hymn** (the Quiet Age): Ysmerel says the Choir cut it to stop the ache; the Archivist says it was cut by *one hand*; the Dissenter says it was never cut — "it was *withdrawn*, which is a different act."
2. **The Night of the Seven Doors:** Calend says he held the first; Hesk says he locked the last; Ilsabet says the seven were drawn *before* they were built.
3. **The Siege of the First Light:** Oru remembers a blur of pale edges, smiling; Sethane measured it and says it was *small*; the Surveyor says it was *vast* and never happened.
4. **The Great Berth Count:** Dorrow counted the berths and the ships and found them equal; Wim says one is paid by no one; Brannoch's ring is warm.
5. **The Winter of the Tree:** the Keeper says Maren left; Tamsin says Maren was carried; the tree says nothing.

## 6. The Expansion Seams (how this can keep growing, like an Erdtree-scale DLC, without breaking a word)
The locked story is built around **silences**. Each silence is a *seam*: a closed room the main story walks past. Each can open later as a new region with its own cast, quests and reveals, set **alongside** the existing timeline (as *Shadow of the Erdtree* ran beside Elden Ring's, not after it). None needs a locked fact changed.

| Seam | The silence it opens | A region it could be | New cast it brings | What it adds | What it must never say |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S-I The Unmade Hour** | "the hour made twice" (Sixth Hour) | the first Sixth Hour, before the second | the first Mercy; the made who *weren't kept* | what unmaking felt like from inside | why it was made twice |
| **S-II The Long Morning** | the Uncounted's home, before the hole | a world of light with no horizon | the Uncounted's people; Orsolya's flake | what the created record looked like | whether the Uncounted is Eternity |
| **S-III The Road to the Seam** | Teru's walk | a long road under the Station | Teru; Mara as a girl; the first Wardens | what "someone who isn't here" meant | where Teru ended |
| **S-IV The Siege of the First Light** | Oru's blur | a battlefield made of pale edges | Oru young; his army of students | what the First Morning was defended from | Oru's real age |
| **S-V The Forty-Second** | the one who turned back | the village the Many-Handed left | the Forty-Second; Kith's line | what staying costs | whether staying was right |
| **S-VI The Unlit Hulls** | the six ships + the seventh | the yard where Soumaya's hull was laid | the shipwrights; the first nameplate | who built the seventh hull and why blank | the name |
| **S-VII The Faded Ward, Entire** | Tamsin's half-returned | a ward that is a whole world | the Faded in full; Ruel; Pip; Linnet | how the Hush returns people | whether they all return |
| **S-VIII The Folded Letter** | the one sentence | an "ending" region the player reaches only by choice | the Folded One | the sentence's *reader* (never its words) | whether it is kind |

**DLC shape (my proposal, not a promise):** one seam per expansion, each introduced by one new Item Reaction or rumour in the existing world (a seed in the base game), each adding **~30 entries, ~6 canon people with threads, 1 region with 6–10 landmarks with passages, and 2–3 relics.** Because every addition is an entry, a person, a place passage or a thread in the existing data model, no engine work is needed beyond a new world scene.

## 7. How this reaches the player (mapped to what exists)
- **Entries (the Book):** each [NEW] fact with an object becomes one entry with a trigger (spot / gift / relic), checked by the web checker. Target for this draft: ~45 entries (about 35 objects/sights, ~10 echoes).
- **Threads (the cast):** deepen each existing thread by 2–3 steps and add **Mara, Pip's trail (Hesk), Linnet (Tamsin), Aldous (Ysmerel)** as people you *meet through others*, never directly.
- **Place passages:** one new passage per landmark that gained a tie (the gate-post lamp, the ward, the hymnal alcove).
- **Item Reactions:** each person reacts to whatever you hold that lies on one of their edges (this is where the web becomes felt — the same coat in three rooms).
- **A lineage view in the Book:** a second sky of the seven Lines; a person lights when you have found *anything of theirs*; a Line's thread lights when two of its people are lit. No counts, no "complete".
- **Rumours:** short arrival lines drawn from an edge ("There's a lamp at the gate-post nobody pays for.").
- **Pictures:** each new entry may get an image after an approved prompt (Muse), as for the first four.
