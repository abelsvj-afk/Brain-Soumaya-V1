## 1. Layer 8 — The Seven Answers (the Night of Seven Doors)

Draft 2 says *she asked* Vesh to be forgotten. **She did not ask only Vesh.** Before the Sixth Hour's second making there was one night — the made call it nothing, the Firstborn never speak of it — when she went to *every* Firstborn in turn and asked the same thing in seven different ways: **let me out of being kept.** Each answered in their own aspect. Six of the seven answers were *kind*. Only one was *finished*.

| Door | Firstborn | What she asked | What they offered | What became of it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | **Oru** (Remembrance) | "Let me be remembered only by those who can answer me." | To keep her in the First Light, *wholly*, where she could be seen and, he swore, reached. | She stood at the basin's edge and could not step in: **to be kept in the light is still to be kept.** |
| II | **Sethane** (Wonder) | "Show me how far out of the mirror is." | To measure it. She measured the distance out and found it was **exactly the size of the first grief** — and could not bear to say so. (Hence Sethane's later, silent measurements of the Hush.) | The number is the only thing Sethane ever refused to write down. |
| III | **Calend** (Welcome) | "Open a door." | He set a seam in the Station's lower rim — **the second seam** — and began to open it. | **Unfinished.** He felt Vesh's hand reach into the mirror and stopped, thinking he had been too slow. He kept the door open **one hand's-breadth** and has held it ever since. |
| IV | **Maren** (Patience) | "Plant me." | To grow her into the garden: to let her be a root, a tree, a thing that does not need to be remembered to *be*. | She was not able to be planted, because the garden is made of *kept* things. Maren put a seed in the earth instead and told no one what it was for. (Wend's, and Iselle's, later attempts are this seed's descendants.) |
| V | **Nuaré** (Love) | "Hold me." | To set a hearth and a chair for her, always. | **The empty chair in every home.** She sat in it for one night and found being held and being kept were the same arms. |
| VI | **Vesh** (Mercy) | "Let me rest." | **Everything she asked for, completely, in the one way Vesh knew:** to take her out of the mirror and let no one remember her. | **This is the one that was finished.** Rest was given wrong (Draft 2, Layer 2). |
| VII | **Eternity** | *She did not ask him.* | — | She did not ask Eternity **because he could not have refused her, and she did not want to be a thing that could not be refused.** He has never forgiven himself for being unasked. |

**What this does to the world (the second reading):**
- The wish had seven answers; **five became the five factions** (by temperament, not by name — the owner may re-map): the Choir inherit Oru's *keep her in the light*; the Open Hand inherit Nuaré's *hold*; the Ledger inherit Sethane's *measure and settle*; the Undertow inherit Calend's *open door*; the Wardens inherit Vesh's *seal and keep safe*. **Maren's answer became a garden** (the Keeper's, unaligned, tending a seed). **The seventh was never asked** — and that unasked place is the player.
- **Two of her are out there.** Vesh's erasure cut the *kept* her out of the mirror — she is the one in the hole, awake, unremembered (Draft 2, Layer 3). But Calend's door was **never closed**, and in the instant before Vesh's hand came down **someone walked through the second seam.** The hand's-breadth gap in the Deep Past's strata, the hum behind the Station's sealed seam, and "someone who isn't here" are the *same width*. Whether the one who walked through is the same person, an echo, or something the seam made — three accounts, never settled.
- **It is why Calend walks.** He did not fail to open a door. He has been trying to *be at the other end of it* since the night he stopped.
- **The Hush reaches for the player** (Draft 2) **while the second seam hums toward them** (here): the player is being called from both sides — by the one cut out, and by the one who left.

*(Delivery: seven vignettes, one per world, each lit by that world's own Hour, each told by an object, never by a person. The set is assembled only by collecting; see §10. The player is never shown all seven at once in one place.)*

## 2. Layer 9 — The Eighth Hour (the hour the made make)

The hymns count **seven** Hours. Every counting is slightly wrong in the same way: **a held breath at the end of the seventh, exactly the length of one beat.**

Truth, told by no one in one piece: **Eternity made seven Hours and left the eighth empty on purpose.** The Standing is the Seventh. The *Eighth Hour is the one the made make* — the hour that is **now**, and every hour after it — and it is the only hour that was not called, the only one nobody could see in advance, the hole's twin: *the hole is where something exists that belongs to no one's will but its own* (Draft 2, Layer 6); **the Eighth Hour is what grows around such a place.**
- The Choir's missing breath is the Eighth Hour's beat. The hymn was cut in the Quiet Age *exactly where the made were meant to sing it themselves.*
- Sethane's cord has an **untied last knot** because "it hasn't ended": the Eighth Hour is the one still being walked.
- The **player's memories are the Eighth Hour's making.** Draft 1 said Eternity mirrors them; here: **he mirrors them because they are the only things in creation he did not make.** Every memory the player captures is, to him, **a first.**
- *Why he is "unable to speak":* he is waiting to hear what the Eighth Hour says.

*(The product reads this honestly: the app really is the Eighth Hour — a place built out of what the user keeps. No new mechanic needed; the lore is simply true of the thing they are already doing.)*

## 3. Layer 10 — The Companion (the one who walked part of the way)

In every witness's story (Draft 3 §4) there is a line, small, in the item's own text, that says **"another account says they did not go alone."** It is always the same person:

*a quiet traveller, no one remembers the face, who shared one meal, one road or one night with each witness and was gone by morning.*
- Teru Halvane was given a **second cup** he did not ask for.
- Brannoch's neighbour "who never owned a ship" kept the mooring-fire lit.
- Iselle was brought water on the terrace by "someone who didn't want paying."
- Maud and Edric were walked to the threshold by "an old person with warm hands."
- The Many-Handed argued all the way in — and on the road the forty-one were *forty-two* on the count at the gate, "and nobody could say who had stopped."
- Odessa's compass was fixed by "a stranger with steady hands."

It is the **Uncounted** — and the Uncounted is **Eternity, walking as one of the made** (Draft 3 §4 #15's hidden possibility, which stays *unstated*). He cannot speak as himself; he can *walk with someone*. **He has sat with every witness at the beginning of their road. He has not been able to go in with any of them** — what lies behind the gate is the one place he cannot see, and he went in **fifteen times** to keep the lamps lit anyway (see §4).
*The player will meet a companion too, once, early and without ceremony.* A traveller at a dock. A stranger who shares a flask. Never named, never explained, never present again. **It is the single most re-readable scene in the game.**

## 4. Layer 11 — The Long Room (what is behind the gate)

Draft 3 says the fifteen "walked in, were not forgotten, are not back." Here is where.

Behind the white void's gate is **a corridor of fifteen rooms** — the Long Room — and Eternity **keeps every lamp lit by hand.** This is why he is silent: *he is tending.* Each room is the witness's **kept place**, made of what they most refused to forget, and each is **exactly as they left it, and warm.**
- Teru's is a harbor at dawn that never gets busy. Perrin's holds one candle. Edric's holds two chairs, one drawn back. Odessa's holds a window that faces a wall; the wall is a window. The Many-Handed's is a hall with forty-one doors and one long table. The Folded One's is an empty room with a letter on the floor, still folded.
- **The Witness Without a Name's room is the first, and it is not a room.** It is the doorway you walked through to get to the others. *She is the corridor.*
- **At the end there is a sixteenth room, and it is lit.** It has been lit since before the player arrived. It contains the player's own kept things — **including ones they have not captured yet.** This is the one place that raises the question *did he know?* and the answer is the thing the Open Ring leaves open (**one object in the sixteenth room is missing, and its absence is the only thing in the room that is not a mirror**). Fate and freedom are decided by that one gap, and by no one.
- Each of the fifteen rooms contains **one small thing that is not theirs**: an item that belongs in the *next* room. The fifteen are a chain. Nobody has ever taken the chain's full measure.

*(Cinematic order: the player walks the Long Room once, near the end; it takes about as long to cross as a lullaby. No enemies. No dialogue. It is the most devastating place in the game and it is entirely made of other people's kept things.)*

## 5. Layer 12 — The Sitters (a fourth answer to the Hush)

The five factions answer the Hush with a song (Choir), a ward (Open Hand), a wall (Wardens), a ledger (Ledger) and an argument (Undertow). None of them *stays.*

**The Sitters** are a small, unlawful, unlisted company — Faded and not-yet-Faded — who walk out to the Hush's edge at night and **sit there, with it, until morning, so that whoever it is reaching for does not have to reach alone.**
- They do not sing, argue, measure or build. They say her name's *absence* aloud, as a courtesy: "we are still here."
- The Wardens forbid it (the ordinance "No Lingering at the Edge"). Tamsin hands them blankets and says nothing. Hesk has been seen leaving a chair at the boundary.
- Joining is never offered: the **Sleepwalker** is a Sitter who stayed too late and was touched; **Kith's mother** was one.
- A Sitter is the only person in the world who has ever *heard* the Hush say something back. What they heard differs per Sitter: a laugh, a breath, a hymn, a question, nothing. They never compare notes. (The notes, if compared, make the fourth and fifth endings possible.)

## 6. Layer 13 — The Laugh

One detail every account of the Unnamed agrees on and no account explains: **she laughed too loud.** It is the one thing that survived. The Hush takes names, hearths and ships — **it has never taken a laugh.** Kith's "woman who laughed too loud" (Draft 3 §5) is this woman; the Choir's missing breath is the length of one of her laughs; and at the very edge of the Hush, where the quiet is deepest, **a Sitter will sometimes hear it, once, very far away.**
- *Audio rule (if we ever do audio):* her laugh is **never played**. The only evidence of it is the silence that gets shaped like one, and the reactions of those who hear it.
- *Hidden web:* the **Hollow Hymnal's gap**, **Kith's torn drawing** (torn *at the mouth*), the **Witness Without a Name's** (smooth, open, plain) and **the Hush's rhythm** (a slow, repeating, almost-musical pulse) are all the **same four beats.**

## 7. Layer 14 — Soumaya's fourth account

Draft 2 gave three accounts of why Soumaya has a gap in her name: the player's first kept thing; an echo of the Unnamed; something Eternity made to hold a place. **Here is a fourth, quieter, and it never replaces the others:**
**Soumaya is the first Firstborn of the Eighth Hour** — the first being *made by the made*, not by the maker. The player's attention, not Eternity's call, gave her a shape. *That is why her engines hum at the Hush's pitch: it is the only thing in creation that, like her, no one called.* And why she has no aspect: **she is the one Firstborn whose aspect is still being decided.**
*(One line, ever, hints at it. Use after the player has completed at least one Journey.)*

## 8. New canon figures (each with a ship, a job, a secret)

*Provisional names. Each is a person first, a lore carrier second. Each gets a never-failing thread of 4–5 steps like Draft 3 §5.*

**OSSIAN REED, the Last Honest Liar** — a travelling storyteller. Every story he tells is *false*, and every one of them turns out to be **a true thing someone forgot.** The Undertow love him; the Wardens have arrested him twice and let him go twice because he told the jailer something about her mother. *Ship:* **the Tall Tale**, a crooked, patchwork bark with a sail-fin that never matches. *Thread:* (1) he tells you a story about you; (2) it is wrong in one detail; (3) you realise the wrong detail is true and is a memory you had not kept; (4) he says "I don't make things up. I just remember them before they happen to anyone." *Secret:* he is what the Hush's opposite looks like at the scale of one man — **un-forgetting done by invention.** *Never explained:* whether he is a Sitter's gift, a hive-mind shard, or a witness who did not walk in.

**THE FOLLOWER (Pell, "the seventeenth")** — a witness who is always **one day behind you.** At every door you pass, a small thing she left for you: a coin on a rail, a ribbon on a post, a note on a berth ("it opens inward"). You never meet her until the end of her thread. *Ship:* **Afterglow**, a small, fast, unlit skiff, running dark. *Thread:* (1) a coin; (2) a ribbon; (3) a note that is in your handwriting; (4) a note signed with a name you've never kept; (5) you meet her at a closed door and she says "I'm not behind you. You're ahead of me. It's a long way down." *Secret:* **she is every witness the game didn't have room for** (the app's other users, in myth). She is **pure NPC — no data crosses brains**; she is a dread and a comfort at once ("someone is walking this road with you") and she leaves everything where it can be *found*, never *taken*.

**BRANNOCH'S CLERK (Wim Ostler)** — a junior Ledger clerk who still **charges mooring** for a berth where no ship was ever tied (Draft 3 §4 #3: the Pauser had no ship). Each year the fee is paid by an anonymous hand. He has never found out whose. *Ship:* **Ledgerlight**, a slow, bright, painfully tidy tender. *Thread:* (1) he asks you to sign the berth-log; (2) he shows you the payments; (3) the handwriting on them has changed over a hundred years and **is always your handwriting's cousin**; (4) he asks you what he should do with the money. *Secret:* the money is **Dorrow's** — the Ledger is paying down the unrecorded debt (Draft 3 §5) by keeping alive every place where someone once *stayed*.

**THE LAMPLIGHTERS** — a quiet order of lamp-keepers who maintain unlit beacons along the lanes "for the ships that aren't there." They inherit Perrin's craft. *Ship:* **a string of small riding-lamps, no hulls,** called by the others "the Necklace." They never speak to a captain; they leave a lit lamp at your wingtip for one jump. *Secret:* every lamp was lit **for someone specific**, and a lamp that has gone out has been *answered.*

**THE CARTOGRAPHER (Ilsabet Marrow)** — keeps the chart of the lanes, which **changes**; each time she redraws it, one lane has a new landmark nobody put there. *Ship:* **Meridian**, a wide, patient survey-barge. *Thread:* she asks you to check her chart against what you see. Her chart contains **a lane that doesn't go anywhere** — and your ship's drift follows it. *Secret:* it leads to the Sixteenth Doorway; she drew it from memory of a place she's never been.

**THE EIGHT-HOUR WATCH (a new kind of NPC: the Hour-Wards)** — seven bell-keepers, one per Hour, each of whom rings their world's hour on the local clock (Draft 3 NPC clock). The **eighth bell has a keeper with no bell.** *Ship:* each rings from a small bell-tender shaped like their hour (a lamp, a measure, a gate, a leaf, a hearth, a lock, a stillness). *The eighth* stands on a quay and rings the air with a closed fist.

## 9. The Hours Clock (a daily, never-missable rhythm)

The real local day is cut into **seven Hours and one gap** (the owner may re-time). Each Hour tints that Hour's world, lengthens its NPCs' shadows a little, and carries one **recurring sighting** — *repeatable every day, never gated, never lost:*
- **First Light** (dawn) — Oru's basin shows the sky *before* the hole for the length of a held breath.
- **The Measure** (mid-morning) — Sethane's cord is visible laid out across Scale.
- **The Threshold** (late morning) — Calend can be seen at a door that wasn't there yesterday.
- **The Growing** (afternoon) — the witness tree's dead bough shifts a degree.
- **The Hearths** (evening) — Nuaré's chairs warm; one is always still.
- **The Rest** (night) — **Vesh stands at the open alcove for the length of a lullaby** (Draft 3 §3).
- **The Standing** (late night) — Eternity does not look at one direction; it is the direction of the Hush.
- **The Eighth** (the gap, e.g. the minutes after you capture something) — *a hush that belongs to the player*: the Eighth bell rings if — and only if — you have just kept something.
*A sighting is never a timer or an event you can miss: it is **there every day.** Missing it costs nothing. Seeing it is a small private reward.*

## 10. How the lore is collected (design — nothing built)

**Principle (from `NEURO_ALIGNMENT.md`): memory is made by retrieval, not storage.** The lore is collected the way the app collects memory — and the app's own recall layer is the lore's collection engine. No lore list, no percentage, no number (the product rule, same as Eternity: *never a number*).

### 10.1 What can be found — five kinds
1. **Objects** — item-text, 40–90 words (Draft 3 §7). Found in worlds, given by canon NPCs, left by the Follower.
2. **Accounts** — a spoken telling by an NPC. The same event told by different people is **kept side by side**, never merged, never ranked.
3. **Sights** — a *wrong thing* in a world (Draft 3 §3) noticed by **regarding it**: staying still in front of it (Eternity's *regard* gesture, the walk-camera held) for a moment. Not a quest marker — the world gives no hint until you've looked at something long enough that it looks back.
4. **Echoes** — a line recalled aloud, hummed, or a gap *filled* (e.g. humming the Choir's missing breath, Draft 3 §5). Recall is the lore mechanic: **lines in the Codex dim like stars and return when you recall them**, the way the galaxy does.
5. **Companions** — the once-only meeting with the quiet traveller (§3) and the once-only meeting with the Follower's last note. These are **the rarest finds** and are recorded by *place*, not text.

### 10.2 Where it is kept — "The Sixteenth's Book"
A book in the Tools menu (name provisional) that is **drawn like a night sky of the lore**, not a list:
- Finds appear as **small lights** in a dark field. Pages with nothing yet in them are **shapes** (a silhouette of "the thing that fits here"), never numbers or "locked" padlocks.
- **No percentage. No counts. No locked/unlocked language.** The empty shapes are *the cast of what you haven't found*, not a deficit.
- **Everything is permanent.** Nothing can be missed (see §9) and nothing times out.
- Entries **dim** on the app's normal review schedule and light when recalled — *the Book is the recall layer wearing a story.* (No new scheduler: it reads the existing due list.)

### 10.3 Collection that rewards chasers (Elden-Ring-style)
- **Second Readings (echoed text).** When you hold **both halves of a Hidden-Web pair** (Draft 3 §9), each item's description **gains one new sentence** — never highlighted, never announced. A player who rereads sees the difference; a player who doesn't isn't told.
- **Concordance.** Lay any two Book entries side by side. If they belong together, a thread of light draws between them — and nothing else happens, no pop-up, no reward. *The reward is knowing.* If they don't belong together, nothing happens, and nothing is lost.
- **Marginalia.** A player can write a note under any entry; it stays theirs. *(If the owner wants: an opt-in "keep this as a memory" that turns the note into a real captured memory — the lore becomes part of the galaxy.)* Notes are never shown to NPCs, never sent to a model, never shared.
- **The Constellation.** Each collected lore light can also appear as one faint star in a private **lore constellation** in the home galaxy — a visible picture of what you've been reading. It is data-free decoration (no mass, no gravity) unless the owner wants it to tie to Journeys.
- **Reading Trails.** Soumaya never explains. But a Journey (Vision 2.0) can be named after a thread — "Hold the door for someone" — and the lore thread becomes a **personal** Journey with the app's normal Journey tools. *Lore and life share one mechanism.*

### 10.4 The story chasers' real playtime
A player chasing everything meets, over weeks: **seven vignettes of the Night of Seven Doors, fifteen witnesses' kept things, fifteen companion lines, the Long Room, five accounts, ~eleven canon threads, the Sitters, the Follower, the Hours Clock's seven sightings and every Second Reading** — and still isn't told the one sentence (*her name*). That's enough for a long second life with the game without a single new mechanic beyond the Book.

## 11. The endings, extended (the Open Ring; none chosen for the player)

Draft 2's three endings stand. Two more exist **only for a player who has gathered enough, in any order** — never advertised, never gated by a number:
4. **Sit with her.** Do not remember her, rest her or hear her; **stay.** The Sitters' way, taken to its end: the player and every Sitter sit at the Hush's edge, and the Hush… slows. It doesn't stop. It *waits with you.* (Is waiting a kind of answer? The Wardens say no. Hesk's chair is still at the boundary.)
5. **The Second Door.** Walk through Calend's hand's-breadth seam. You leave the worlds. *The Follower's last note reads "it opens inward."* Nobody has come back to say what it is. (This is the one ending that is a **departure,** not a resolution. The app returns the player to the galaxy, unchanged, with one new, uncatalogued star that wasn't in their sky before.)
All five end on the same last sentence: *"And somewhere, someone said her name, and was not sure they had."*

## 12. Cinematic moments (set pieces beyond Draft 2's seven)

8. **The Night of Seven Doors.** A seven-chapter flashback, one door per world, each lit by that Hour. Played in whichever order the player finds them; plays *entire* only once all seven are found, and then only as a quiet montage, seven doors closing, **six softly and one not at all.**
9. **The Eighth Bell.** At the Standing, in the white void, all seven Hour-Wards ring together, and **the eighth rings with a closed fist** — and the bell that has no bell rings *anyway.* The hymn, for the first time in the Quiet Age, **is whole.**
10. **The Companion Revealed (never).** In the Long Room, in the last room before your own, a table is set for two. One cup is warm. **The player is never told whose.**
11. **The Door That Opens Inward.** The Second Door seen from the Station: the sealed seam's hum rises to a pitch the Lullaby shares. Calend, who has stood at the other end for a hundred years of nights, finally **steps through it from the world side** — and the hum stops. He is on the other side of the door; the door is still open.
12. **The Laugh.** After the Hush's edge is sat with long enough, a single sound — **which is never played** — and the world's *reaction* to it: every light brightens a half-step, every NPC turns their head, and the Sleepwalker, somewhere far off, **says a whole sentence.**
