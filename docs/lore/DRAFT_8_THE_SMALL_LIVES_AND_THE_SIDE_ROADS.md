# DRAFT 8 — The Small Lives and the Side Roads

*PROPOSED by Claude, 2026-10-31, from the owner's request: "add lore again one more time … enough where side quests are possible … plenty of lore … add more to the individual lives of each person affected by that lore and affected by the world and living in it … a few more NPCs flying around too."*
**Status: PROPOSED — additive only.** Draft 7 is LOCKED (see its header, including the ruling *nothing dies, things rest*). Nothing here changes a locked word; every item is a new room off an existing corridor. Where I add people, they are tagged **[NEW]**; where I add to someone already canon, **[+]**.

**What this draft is for.** Drafts 1–7 are the *mountain*: the great hurts and the people who carry them. This draft is the *foothills*: the ordinary lives the mountain casts a shadow over, and the **roads** (side quests) that run between them. In a game like Elden Ring the main story is mostly felt through the people who live in its ruin — the merchant who sells you a thing and has a reason, the knight guarding a bridge for a king who is gone. That is what this is.

**Side-road rules (so every road is safe to leave):**
1. A road has a **Giver**, a **small ask**, three or four **steps** (a conversation, a place, a thing), and a **Warmth** at the end. It **rests** if you leave it — the Giver says so kindly and is still there. Nothing fails, nothing expires, nothing costs.
2. Every road **touches the web**: it faces one of the locked edges (Draft 7 §3, W1–W24) from a *new side*, so finishing it makes the main lore feel closer without explaining it.
3. A road's **gift** is never power. It is one of: a Book entry · a place passage · a new line of Item Reaction · a small object that rests in the Book · a person who now greets you differently.
4. No road requires another. A player who walks none still has a living world; a player who walks all is still not told.

## 1. The Roads (side quests), by world — 36 of them
*Format: **Name** — Giver · premise · steps (…) · touches · gift.*

### The Great Station
1. **The Lamp Nobody Pays For** — **Mara Halvane [NEW]** (the gate-post lamp) · she asks you, once, to *check the oil*, as though it were nothing. Steps: find the lamp at the gate-post; notice it is warm; ask the harbor controller whose berth it is (she has no record); return and say nothing — Mara pours the oil. Touches W2/W1 (Teru, Calend). Gift: *the Gate-post Lamp* (an entry) and Mara greets you by name.
2. **A Door Closed** — **Hesk** · a docking inspection that becomes a question: "Have you ever shut a door on someone who needed it open?" Steps: the inspection; a walk to the annexes; Hesk shows you the one door he keeps shut; he asks you to *not tell Calend*. Touches W3/W4. Gift: Hesk's Ring of Keys (entry: one key with no door).
3. **The Seventh Berth** — **Wim Ostler [NEW]** · he cannot reconcile the berth-log: one berth is paid, never occupied. Steps: count berths with him; find the empty cradle (the Drydock Gantry); find the payment slip with no name; Wim gives you the slip to keep. Touches W11/W10. Gift: *The Unclaimed Slip*.
4. **What the Wayfarer Left in the Coat** — **Calend** · a patch on his coat has come loose; he asks you to hold it while he rests. Steps: carry the patch to three places that each *half-match* its colour; the third match is a place you have not been. Touches the unfinished patch (Draft 7). Gift: a Book line: *"The patch is the colour of someone who has not arrived."*
5. **The Tall Tale's Second Verse** — **Ossian Reed [NEW]** · he tells you a story with one deliberate error and dares you to find it. Steps: hear it; ask three people for their version; the error is the same wrong detail in each; he grins. Touches W13 (Pell). Gift: Item Reaction: Ossian reacts to the Follower's Ribbon.
6. **The Cartographer's Margin** — **Ilsabet Marrow [NEW]** · she is drawing a lane past the seam and wants a witness to *stand where she cannot*. Steps: stand at the lower rim, be still; report what you hear (the hum); she redraws. Touches W14. Gift: *Marrow's Lane* (entry).

## 2. The Small Lives — who lives in the shadow of all this
*Ordinary people. None has a quest of their own; each is **met** (a name tag, a line, a routine) and each is tied to the mountain by one thread, so the world feels lived-in by people who did not choose its grief.*

**The Station**
- **Brisa the ferrywoman** — runs a tender between berths; counts passengers aloud; always counts one fewer than boarded and corrects herself, embarrassed.
- **Old Tomas of the annex bar** — wipes the same glass; the glass has no rim-mark on one side where someone used to drink.
- **Lysa, a Warden cadet** — Hesk's driller; has never seen the locked room and is afraid of what is in it.
- **Pod, the berth-boy** — takes parcels for ships that don't dock; keeps a tin whistle; plays the same four notes.
- **A fuel-seller's widow, Orin Vale** — buys Dorrow's fuel and sells it again at cost; no one knows why.

**The Deep Past**
- **The Dig-Warden (Marta Quill)** — counts strata; one short, always.
- **Stone-Reader Eno** — reads the rim with his thumb; will not read the sixth layer.
- **A child of the dig, Fen** — draws the layers in chalk and always draws seven.

**The Quiet / Archive**
- **Brother Anselm the binder** — repairs torn pages; will not re-insert one.
- **A scribe, Hollis** — copies Noor's question in ink and cannot stop.
- **Sister Tevey of the Choir** — hums the held breath in her sleep.

**Scale**
- **Cadet Rho of the Plumb** — learns the cord knots; fond of the untied one.
- **A lamplighter, Sennet** — tends the Necklace; keeps one lamp lit for no one.

**The Growing Place**
- **Gardener Joss** — waters the terraces at dawn; skips one terrace and does not say why.
- **Teller Maud-Anne** — keeps the garden's bees; names each hive after someone who walked in.

**People**
- **Baker Corvin** — leaves a loaf at one table no one sits at.
- **The twins, Arlo and Nell** — play a game where one is always "the one who went away" (a game Kith will not play).
- **Seamstress Piet** — sews buttons onto coats and keeps a jar of spares (for Tamsin).

**Everywhere**
- **The Crier** — walks the worlds calling the hours in a voice no choir would use.
- **A pilot, Reva Skell** — ferries goods between worlds; swears she once overtook herself.
- **The Postman who never delivers** — carries letters addressed to "the one who is here."

*Use in-app:* these are **ambient presence** (a name tag, one or two lines, a routine on the same clock as the cast), not full cast — cheap to add, expensive to forget. They are the difference between a stage with seven actors and a town.

## 5. How all of this reaches the app (honest map of what exists vs. what must be built)
- **Already there:** cast + clock + dialogue + Item Reactions + the Book + spots + place passages + pictures. New canon people are **data** in those systems.
- **Built in the same change as this draft:** the six new canon NPCs (cast rows, dialogue threads with their pointers, Item Reactions), **Pell's one-day lag**, and the **ambient small lives** as presence-only entries where the engine allows. 
- **Not built yet (needs its own spec):** a **road tracker** (what road you are on and which step), so roads can be offered, rested and resumed; the **lineage view**; the Draft 7 entries and graph edges (the Station, Archive and Ward slices); voicing; ambient traffic ships.
- **Order for the rest:** Slice A (Station knot) entries + roads 1–6 → road tracker → Slice B (Archive) + roads 11–15 → Slice C (the Ward) + roads 24–28 → Garden/Measure/Dawn → lineage view → the **eight seams, one at a time**, in this order (my choice, each shippable): **S-VII The Faded Ward, Entire** (the saddest; explains Kith/Tamsin/Hesk) → **S-III The Road to the Seam** (Teru/Mara/Calend) → **S-V The Forty-Second** (Kith's line) → **S-I The Unmade Hour** → **S-IV The Siege of the First Light** → **S-VI The Unlit Hulls** → **S-II The Long Morning** → **S-VIII The Folded Letter** (always last: it is the end that is a choice).
- **Everything above keeps the product rules:** no score, no timer, no loss, no punishment; roads rest; nothing is required.
