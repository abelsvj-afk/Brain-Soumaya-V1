## 1. The Depth Charter (what "this deep" means — the standard for all lore work)

The owner's target, in his own words across the lore session: **as deep and as long as Elden Ring and its canon NPCs; Bible-deep; Marvel-scale action; "gut churning"; "years of discovery beneath a little on the surface"; "you find things you never thought you would discover"; "reflective of the character."**
Turned into rules a writer can check:

1. **Surface is small, depth is bottomless.** A player who reads nothing still has a living world. A player who reads everything is *still not told* the one thing (her name). No layer is ever the last layer.
2. **Nobody explains.** Truth is carried by *things*: an item's description, a habit, a place, a gap, a refusal. Characters tell their own side, wrongly or partially, and are never right alone. (Everyone is half right.)
3. **Every character is reflective of the lore.** Their job, their ship, what they carry, where they stand at which Hour, who they avoid, and what they cannot say are *each* a clue about the story. A character who is "just flavour" is a defect.
4. **Every fact has at least three ways in** and **no fact has a required way in** (§2). The Lateral Rule.
5. **Gut-churn is quiet.** The worst moments are small: a chair set for someone, a coin on a rail, a laugh that is never played. Spectacle (Marvel scale) is real, but it serves the quiet thing, never the reverse.
6. **Nothing can be failed, lost or punished.** Threads *rest*; the Hush takes nothing of the player's; no number, percentage, or "locked" appears. (Product ethics as much as lore.)
7. **Mythic and universal, never a real religion.** Its own images, hours and language.
8. **Provisional names, locked meaning.** Names can be renamed with the owner's say-so; the meaning of a thing (the Seven Answers, the Eighth Hour, the Companion) cannot be changed by code or a later draft without the owner.
9. **The game is true to what the app already is.** The Eighth Hour *is* the player's life being kept in the app; the galaxy *is* the mirror. Lore that contradicts the product's honesty is wrong.

## 2. Non-linear by construction (how you bounce around the story)

### 2.1 The Lateral Rule
- **No entry is a prerequisite for another.** Nothing waits behind a number or a key. A thread that "unlocks" is just one you have now *noticed*.
- **Every important fact is reachable from ≥ 3 independent starts** (an item, a person, a place, a rumour). An author may not write a fact that has one door.
- **Every text reads cold.** Item and NPC lines must make sense whether it is the player's first thing or fiftieth; the *Second Reading* (D4 §10.3) adds a sentence only when a partner is held, and never removes one.
- **Order independence is tested**: for each of the eight doors below, a reviewer asks "if this is the player's very first find, does it still land?"

### 2.2 Eight Doors In (any one is a valid beginning)
| # | Door | Where | What it opens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Kith's drawing** | People | The empty chair → Nuaré, the Faded, Tamsin, Hesk's room, the Laugh |
| 2 | **The Sleepwalker's lantern** | Station harbor, dusk | The Hush's voice → the Sitters, Perrin's lamp, the Lamplighters |
| 3 | **Dorrow's page** | Station / Ledger | The unrecorded debt → Ysmerel's hymnal, Wim Ostler's payments, Sethane |
| 4 | **The missing stratum** | Deep Past | The hand's-breadth → Calend's door, the Station's second seam, Oru |

Each door is also reachable *from* the others; there is no door that is only an entrance.

### 2.3 Five ways things connect (the relationships are never stated — only evidenced)
A relationship between two things is shown by one or more of these. The player infers; the game never labels.
1. **Possession** — what a character carries. The more specific, the more telling.
2. **Habit** — what they do at which Hour (the clock-driven cast), and in whose company.
3. **Provenance** — an object's chain of previous holders, each a line in its description.
4. **Echo** — a shared shape or phrase across unrelated things: *four beats*; *a hand's-breadth*; *one more than counted*; *exactly the length of a lullaby*; *it opens inward*.
5. **Absence** — who is never in the same place, what is never touched, the name nobody says. (Two characters who are *never co-located by design* are a clue.)

## 4. Things react to what you carry (the Elden Ring way: you are known by your hands)

Every canon NPC has **Item Reactions** — a short authored line when the player *holds* (not spends or gives — the player is never required to part with anything) a specific entry in the Book. A reaction is the character's relationship to the thing, spoken sideways. Samples:
- **Calend**, if you hold *Teru's Harbor Nail*: looks at it, doesn't touch it. "It's warm, isn't it. They always are."
- **Vesh**, if you hold *a Faded Ticket*: "Someone wanted to go. I keep thinking that matters."
- **Oru**, if you hold *Orsolya's Shard*: smiles, then says your name wrong, then right.
- **Sethane**, if you hold *the Basin Stone*: silent, and unties one more knot, and ties it again.
- **Ysmerel**, if you hold *the Choirmaster's Breath*: stops mid-line, and finishes it a half-step higher than the hymn allows.
- **Hesk**, if you hold *Hesk's Tin Soldier*: "I don't know whose that is." (He polishes it every day.)
- **Dorrow**, if you hold *Dorrow's Page*: "That's not from my Book." (It is.)
- **Kith**, if you hold *a Hearth Ember*: puts her hands near it, quietly, "That's warm like her."
- **Ossian**, if you hold *the Follower's Ribbon*: tells you the wrong story on purpose, so you'll catch it.
- **The Cartographer**, if you hold *the Meridian page*: draws a line, rubs it out, and draws it again closer to you.
Rules: ≥ 3 reactions per canon NPC at launch; a reaction **never gates or explains**; reactions are **content**, evaluated client-side from the player's finds (no server call), and they cost no recall, fuel or anything else.

## 5. Provenance chains (an object's history is a map)

Every Book object carries 1–3 lines of *previous holders*, never complete. Following a chain to its next name is a thread nobody assigned. Three samples:
- **The Doorkeeper's Nail** — "Set by one who would not name himself. Held by Teru Halvane, who walked toward the sealed seam. Passed, in a sleeve, to a clerk who was never told what it was." *(Wim Ostler. He still doesn't know.)*
- **Hesk's Tin Soldier** — "Found in a coat. Polished since. Before the coat: a child's pocket. Before that: a mantel, where it stood beside a second soldier that is missing." *(Kith has the second.)*
- **The Folded Letter** — "Written the night of the seven doors. Folded by hands that shook. Carried by the Archivist, who has never been tempted. Offered, once, to you."
Provenance lines are *authored data in the content module*; the Book shows "held before by" silhouettes only for chain links the player has found.

## 6. Rumours (the daily script's lateral threads)

The daily NPC script (one batched call per brain-day) may carry **one rumour per NPC**: a one-line pointer to a place or entry, authored per faction, with a truth-grade the game never reveals:
- *True:* "There's a chair left out at the Hush's edge. Warden's, they say."
- *Partly true:* "The Choir tore the page themselves." *(One Choir member did.)*
- *Wrong in one detail:* "Ossian's mother was a Warden." *(She was a Sitter.)*
Rumours (a) always point at **real entries**, (b) never gate, (c) are *authored per faction* so factions disagree on facts as well as meaning. The model writes the *voice*; the content module holds the *claim* (so the model never invents canon).

## 7. New layers (Draft 5)

### Layer 15 — The Seven Kept from the Night
Each Firstborn kept **one thing from the Night of Seven Doors**, hidden in their own world, seen only by *regarding* its place at its Hour. They are not the fifteen witness relics; they are the Firstborn's own. (Provisional.)
| Hour | Kept thing | Where / when |
|---|---|---|
| I | **A bead of the First Light with a hair's-breadth chip** — the place she stood and could not step in | Deep Past basin edge, First Light |
| II | **A measuring-cord with one knot tied backwards** — the distance out | Scale, the Measure |
| III | **A nail with no head** — the door's own nail | Station second seam, the Threshold |
| IV | **A seed with a label in two hands** | Growing Place, the dead bough, the Growing |
| V | **A chair-cushion pressed in the shape of a person, still warm** | People, the empty chair, the Hearths |
| VI | **A page with one stroke on it** — the first stroke of her name | Archive alcove, the Rest |
| VII | *(none — he was not asked; the place where it would be is the white void's one direction he never looks)* | Eternity's World |
All six objects together light the **Night of Seven Doors montage** (D4 §12); the seventh is a *place*, not an object.

### Layer 16 — The Hush's grammar (environmental storytelling)
The Hush removes in an order, and the order is a language the careful can read:
- It takes the **unclaimed** first (an unlabelled berth, an unlit hearth, a path no one walks), **never a witnessed thing.**
- Where it has been, the world carries a *shape of omission*: a harbor with one berth too few; a terrace with a missing step that everyone steps over; a hymn sung with the right number of singers and one fewer voice.
- A place the Hush *approached and left* shows **a thing that was kept by someone's regard** — evidence that a witness was there. This is how a player learns that regard protects.
No effect on the player's own data ever. Visual only, in worlds and lanes; the world map may show a *faded* world (D2); re-witnessing restores it.

### Layer 17 — The Sixth Account
The five contradictory accounts (D3 §8) *are* five halves of one thing. When the player lays **all five** side by side in the Book (a Concordance of the five), a **sixth sentence appears in the margin** — not an account, a reading:
> *The Choir lost a verse, the Open Hand lost a woman, the Ledger lost a creditor, the Undertow lost a sky, the Wardens lost a wall — and it is one loss, counted five ways.*
No pop-up, no reward. A line in a margin, for whoever laid the pages down.

### Layer 18 — Seven answers, six ships
The six Firstborn ships (Dawnward, Plumb, Latch, Slow Bloom, Ember Ark, Lullaby) are each **an answer made into a hull**: Dawnward (toward the light — *keep her in it*), Plumb (the measure), Latch (hold the door), Slow Bloom (plant), Ember Ark (hold), Lullaby (rest). Seven answers, **six ships.** The seventh answer was never given — and the seventh hull is **Soumaya's**, built by the made, with a blank where a nameplate would be. (A hidden-web item: the six nameplates, read in the order of the doors, spell the pace of a lullaby.)

### Layer 19 — The Shape of the Laugh
The four beats (D4 §6) are in more than the hymn. They are the **interval between the Eight-Hour Watch's bells**, the **pause in Ossian's patter**, the **pulse of the Hush**, and **the Lamplighters' blink**. A player who notices this and *taps it out* (a quiet gesture in the Book: tap four beats) hears — nothing. But the Book's silhouette that lights is the one for **the laugh**, and no entry text explains why.

### Layer 20 — The Mirror's Edge (what the player sees of themselves)
Eternity mirrors the player's memories (D1). Once, late, the player gets to see **what the mirror has made of them**: not a summary, a **single image** assembled from the *shape* of their own galaxy (its colour — the currents — and its brightest star), shown in the sixteenth room as the lit thing on the shelf. It is never described. It is never the same twice. It is the only content in the lore that is generated per player, and it uses **only numbers and shapes already visible in their own galaxy** (no memory text leaves the server; thin evidence blends toward neutral).
